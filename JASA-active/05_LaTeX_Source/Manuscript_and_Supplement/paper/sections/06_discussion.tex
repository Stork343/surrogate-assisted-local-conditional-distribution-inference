\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The surrogate enters through augmentation and, with a correct verification
probability, can change variance without changing the local reference-outcome
distribution being estimated. A shared weight coordinates borrowing across
thresholds, and separate evaluation folds support simultaneous inference.

The observed-data structure is semi-supervised: verified reference
outcomes are labels, and the other units contribute covariates and surrogate
measurements. The inferential task differs from ordinary semi-supervised
prediction. We estimate a feature of the reference-outcome distribution under
selective verification, and the surrogate regression is an efficiency device.
The observable criterion \(2G_h-H_h\) compares Full with the anchor along the
fitted correction path at \(x_0\).

Full has lower mean ISE when the surrogate correction is well aligned.
Adaptive loses some efficiency
in those settings because it estimates the weight on a separate fold. Under
local reversal, the selected weight is almost zero and the Adaptive CDF stays
close to the anchor, while Full has larger error. If the candidate correction
itself vanishes, its
coefficient is weakly identified, but changing that coefficient has negligible
effect on the estimated CDF.

The verified fraction and its local allocation both determine precision. In
\(\pi_M(W)=p_Me_M(W)\), \(p_M\) determines the effective local rate and
\(e_M\) records how verification is distributed near the target. Studies with
equal numbers of reference outcomes may therefore have different precision at
\(x_0\). Simultaneous coverage remains below the nominal level when selective
verification leaves very little local information. The minimum verified
kernel-support count across the three role folds is directly observable; the
simulations flag counts below 10 as low information for simultaneous bands.

The fixed-bandwidth simulations and the synthetic AI study use \(F_{h,0}\),
the population distribution at the declared localization scale; the EPA and
VitalDB reference-sampling studies evaluate the corresponding full-data empirical
benchmark \(F_{h,\mathrm{emp}}\). Bands for \(F_0\) require undersmoothing or
bias correction. Kernel localization is most practical for a low-dimensional
conditioning variable.

The reference outcomes define the distribution of interest; the local transfer
criterion governs how the surrogate enters its estimation.
