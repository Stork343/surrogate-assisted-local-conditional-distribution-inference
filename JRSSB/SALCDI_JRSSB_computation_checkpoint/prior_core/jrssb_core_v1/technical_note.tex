\begin{abstract}
This note develops the first technical component of the proposed revision of
\emph{Surrogate-Assisted Local Conditional Distribution Inference with Scarce Reference Outcomes}.
An explicit CDF example separates integrated-variance minimization from simultaneous-band minimization.
We construct a borrowing rule by lower-bounding a paired Gaussian critical-value gain over a common joint-covariance confidence set.
The resulting certificate controls the probability of selecting a harmful borrowing weight and satisfies an oracle inequality without requiring a unique minimizer.
On a fixed nondegenerate threshold grid, the error in the paired gain is proportional to the borrowing weight times the covariance-estimation error.
An explicit observable covariance confidence ball is provided.
A binary local submodel yields a matching signal-detection obstruction and certificate: the signal scale is the inverse square root of the local verified count, whereas the optimal critical-value gain has its square as the relevant scale.
The attached code reproduces the separation example, checks the paired cancellation, and verifies the submodel certificate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research target and relationship to the submitted manuscript}
The supplied 36-page manuscript defines the local target and AIPW signal in Section 2.1, chooses a common borrowing weight by integrated augmentation variance in Section 2.2, and establishes raw-process simultaneous bands in Section 3.3. Its Section 3.4 gives the covariance cost of augmentation error. These components are retained.
The added object is the \emph{gain in a specified simultaneous-inference criterion}, together with the information required to certify that gain.

Several nearby contributions already cover components of this proposal.
PPI++ develops efficient adaptive borrowing for finite-dimensional parameters \citep{ppi}.
Conditional PPI develops local conditional inference and nonasymptotic and minimax results \citep{ppci}; its revised version dated 29 August 2026 was checked.
Generation-powered inference develops an optimal functional borrowing operator and simultaneous bands \citep{gpi}.
Multi-source PPI directly optimizes asymptotic confidence-region volume \citep{mppi}.
Finite-sample costs of estimating PPI++ weights are studied by \citet{nfl}.
Learning a candidate and then validating a risk claim is also an established idea \citep{ltt}.

Accordingly, neither AIPW augmentation, optimization of a confidence-region size, nor a generic lower-confidence-bound decision is treated here as a new principle.
The technical object pursued here is a \emph{paired, local, distribution-level gain certificate} whose uncertainty vanishes at the anchor and whose weak-signal behavior can be related to local verified information.
An operator that dominates all competing covariance operators already improves probabilities of centered convex Gaussian sets. The separation example below concerns a fitted scalar path which need not admit such a dominating element; it does not contradict operator-efficiency results.

\section{Local scores and a common covariance representation}
Condition on an independent training sample. The fitted candidates $q_y(X)$ and $m_y(W)$ are now fixed, bounded in $[0,1]$, and need not be correctly specified. Write
\[
 D_y=m_y-q_y,\qquad a_{\lambda,y}=q_y+\lambda D_y,\qquad 0\leq\lambda\leq1.
\]
Let $O=(W,R,RY)$, $R\perp Y\mid W$, and let the known verification probability satisfy
$\pi(W)=p e(W)$, with $p>0$ and $e(W)\geq c_e>0$.
For $Z_y=\ind\{Y\leq y\}$,
\begin{align}
 U_{\lambda,y}
 &=q_y+\frac R\pi(Z_y-q_y)
   +\lambda\left(1-\frac R\pi\right)D_y
 =U_{0,y}+\lambda B_y,\label{eq:affine}\\
 B_y&=\left(1-\frac R\pi\right)D_y.
\end{align}
Both $\E(B_y\mid W)=0$ and $\E(U_{\lambda,y}\mid W)=\E(Z_y\mid W)$ hold exactly.

Fix $x_0$, a nonnegative bounded kernel $K_h(X-x_0)$, and $v_h=h^d$. Put
\[
 \mu_h=\E K_h>0,\quad f_h=\mu_h/v_h,\quad
 F_h(y)=\E(K_h Z_y)/\mu_h.
\]
Use a fixed threshold grid $y_1<\cdots<y_J$. Define the $2J$-vector
\begin{equation}
 \Psi(O)=\sqrt{p/v_h}\,K_h
 \begin{pmatrix}U_{0,y_1}-F_h(y_1)\\ \vdots\\U_{0,y_J}-F_h(y_J)\\B_{y_1}\\\vdots\\B_{y_J}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Omega=\E(\Psi\Psi^\top).
 \label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
It has mean zero. With $H_\lambda=(I_J,\lambda I_J)$,
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_\lambda=H_\lambda\Omega H_\lambda^\top
 =\Omega_{00}+\lambda(\Omega_{01}+\Omega_{10})+\lambda^2\Omega_{11}.
 \label{eq:pathcov}
\end{equation}
The usual covariance for the $\sqrt{n p h^d}$-scaled kernel-ratio estimator is $\Sigma_\lambda/f_h^2$. The positive scale $f_h^{-1}$ is common to all weights and therefore cancels in weight selection and the sign of a gain.

For an independent evaluation sample of size $n_E$,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat F_{E,\lambda}(y)=\frac{\mathbb P_E K_h U_{\lambda,y}}{\mathbb P_E K_h},\qquad
 \sqrt{n_E p v_h}(\widehat F_{E,\lambda}-F_h)
 =\frac{n_E^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in E}H_\lambda\Psi(O_i)}{\widehat f_{h,E}},
 \label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat f_{h,E}=\mathbb P_E K_h/v_h$. This identity is exact whenever the denominator is positive.

Let $G_\lambda\sim N(0,\Sigma_\lambda)$ and define
\begin{equation}
 c_\Omega(\lambda)=\inf\{t:\Pp(\|G_\lambda\|_\infty\leq t)\geq1-\alpha\},\qquad
 g_\Omega(\lambda)=c_\Omega(0)-c_\Omega(\lambda).
 \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
The raw band has common half-width $c_\Omega(\lambda)/(f_h\sqrt{n_Epv_h})$ at population calibration. A fixed positive shape $w_j$ can be accommodated by dividing the $j$th coordinate of both score blocks by $w_j$ before defining $\Omega$. Shapes depending on $\lambda$ are excluded from this comparison.

\section{A verifiable separation between two efficiency criteria}
Let $S\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.2)$ and
\[
 Y\mid S=0\in\{1,2\}\text{ with equal probabilities},\qquad
 Y\mid S=1\in\{0,2\}\text{ with equal probabilities}.
\]
Take thresholds $(0,1)$, $q=(0.1,0.5)$, $p=0.1$, independent verification, and the legitimate candidate CDF values
\[
 m(S)=\{0.5S,\;0.5+0.4(S-0.2)\}.
\]
The candidate values are $(0,0.42)$ and $(0.5,0.82)$; setting the CDF to zero below the first support point and to one at and above two completes each distribution.
For $K_h=v_h=\mu_h=1$, enumeration of $(S,Y,R)$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_\lambda=
 \begin{pmatrix}.09&.05\\.05&.25\end{pmatrix}
 +\lambda\begin{pmatrix}-.072&-.0288\\-.0288&0\end{pmatrix}
 +\lambda^2\begin{pmatrix}.036&.0288\\.0288&.02304\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:examplecov}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Integrated variance and band width separate]\label{prop:separation}
The integrated-variance minimizer for the equal-weight grid is $\lambda_{\mathrm{IV}}=25/41$. It decreases $\tr\Sigma_\lambda$ but increases the $95\%$ critical value in \eqref{eq:gain} relative to the anchor.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The trace is $.34-.072\lambda+.05904\lambda^2$, whose minimizer is $25/41$.
At the anchor, the union bound gives
\[
 \Pp(\|G_0\|_\infty>.99)
 \leq2\overline\Phi(.99/.3)+2\overline\Phi(.99/.5)
 =.04867037697<.05.
\]
At $\lambda_{\mathrm{IV}}$, the second marginal variance is $.25856632957$, so
\[
 \Pp(\|G_{\lambda_{\mathrm{IV}}}\|_\infty\leq.99)
 \leq2\Phi\{.99/\sqrt{.25856632957}\}-1
 =.94845626635<.95.
\]
Thus $c_\Omega(0)<.99<c_\Omega(\lambda_{\mathrm{IV}})$. The inequalities have margins far exceeding the numerical integration tolerance used in the attached verification.
\end{proof}

Deterministic bivariate normal integration gives the following additional values. The band-minimizing weight is a numerical optimizer; it is not needed for Proposition~\ref{prop:separation}.

\section{Paired certification over a common confidence set}
Let $\calC_G$ be a nonempty compact confidence set of positive semidefinite joint matrices based on the selection sample, satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \Pp(\Omega\in\calC_G\mid\text{training})\geq1-\delta.
 \label{eq:confidence}
\end{equation}
Define the paired lower bound and borrowing rule by
\begin{equation}
 \underline g_G(\lambda)=\inf_{Q\in\calC_G}\{c_Q(0)-c_Q(\lambda)\},\qquad
 \widehat\lambda\in\argmax_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\underline g_G(\lambda),
 \label{eq:rule}
\end{equation}
where $0\in\Lambda\subseteq[0,1]$. A finite numerical grid always permits a maximizer. For a compact continuum, a measurable maximizer is used when it exists. If the maximum is zero, choose zero.
The same $Q$ must be used for the two critical values inside the braces. In particular, $\underline g_G(0)=0$ exactly.

\begin{theorem}[Certification and an oracle inequality]\label{thm:certificate}
Let $\lambda^*\in\argmax_{\lambda\in\Lambda}g_\Omega(\lambda)$ and define, on the event $\Omega\in\calC_G$,
\[
 r_G(\lambda)=\sup_{Q\in\calC_G}|g_Q(\lambda)-g_\Omega(\lambda)|.
\]
The rule \eqref{eq:rule} satisfies
\begin{align}
 &\Pp\{g_\Omega(\widehat\lambda)<0\mid\text{training}\}\leq\delta,\label{eq:safety}\\
 &0\leq g_\Omega(\lambda^*)-g_\Omega(\widehat\lambda)\leq r_G(\lambda^*)
 \quad\text{on }\{\Omega\in\calC_G\}.\label{eq:oracle}
\end{align}
No uniqueness or curvature of the minimizer is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the confidence event,
\[
 g_\Omega(\widehat\lambda)\geq\underline g_G(\widehat\lambda)
 \geq\underline g_G(0)=0,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:safety}. Also,
\[
 g_\Omega(\widehat\lambda)\geq\underline g_G(\widehat\lambda)
 \geq\underline g_G(\lambda^*)\geq g_\Omega(\lambda^*)-r_G(\lambda^*).
\]
Optimality of $\lambda^*$ gives the other side of \eqref{eq:oracle}.
\end{proof}

A computationally certified lower bound may replace the exact infimum. An ordinary local optimizer for the inner minimization supplies an \emph{upper} bound on that infimum, so its output alone is not a valid safety certificate. Analytic envelopes, globally valid bounds, or a rigorously controlled approximation error are required.

\section{The paired error vanishes at the anchor}
This section provides a fixed-grid refinement of Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}. Let
\[
 \mathcal K_{\kappa,B}=\{Q\succeq0:\|Q\|_\op\leq B,\quad
 H_\lambda QH_\lambda^\top\succeq\kappa I_J\text{ for every }\lambda\in[0,1]\}.
\]
This is a convex compact class. The joint matrix $Q$ itself may be singular: a zero correction block is allowed if the anchor covariance is positive definite.

\begin{lemma}[Smoothness of the Gaussian rectangle quantile]\label{lem:smooth}
For fixed $J$ and $\alpha\in(0,1)$, the map
\[
 q_\alpha(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Quantile}_{1-\alpha}\|N(0,\Sigma)\|_\infty
\]
is twice continuously differentiable on the positive definite cone. Its first and second derivatives are bounded, in the relevant induced operator norms, on $\{\kappa I_J\preceq\Sigma\preceq2B I_J\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $H(t,\Sigma)=\int_{[-t,t]^J}\varphi_\Sigma(z)\,dz$ for $t>0$. Smoothness in $\Sigma$ follows by differentiation under the integral on compact positive definite sets. Writing the integral over $[-1,1]^J$ after the substitution $z=tu$ also establishes smoothness in $t$. The derivative $\partial_tH$ is a sum of integrals of strictly positive Gaussian densities over the faces of the rectangle and is strictly positive.
The quantile is the unique positive solution of $H(t,\Sigma)=1-\alpha$.
The implicit function theorem gives smoothness. Continuity and compactness give finite bounds on the derivatives. For reference, the first directional derivative is
\[
 Dq_\alpha(\Sigma)[E]
 =-\frac{\frac12\int_{[-q,q]^J}\varphi_\Sigma(z)
 \{z^\top\Sigma^{-1}E\Sigma^{-1}z-\tr(\Sigma^{-1}E)\}\,dz}
 {\partial_t H(q,\Sigma)},\qquad q=q_\alpha(\Sigma).
\]
The constants can deteriorate with $J$ or with $\kappa\downarrow0$; the statement does not claim a growing-grid bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Anchor-adaptive perturbation and regret]\label{thm:paired}
There is a finite constant $C=C(J,\alpha,\kappa,B)$ such that, for $Q,Q'\in\mathcal K_{\kappa,B}$,
\begin{equation}
 |g_Q(\lambda)-g_{Q'}(\lambda)|
 \leq C\lambda\|Q-Q'\|_\op,\qquad 0\leq\lambda\leq1.
 \label{eq:paired}
\end{equation}
In particular, suppose $\calC_G\subseteq\mathcal K_{\kappa,B}$ and every member of $\calC_G$ is within $\varepsilon_G$ of a common center. On the confidence event,
\begin{equation}
 0\leq c_\Omega(\widehat\lambda)-\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_\Omega(\lambda)
 \leq2C\lambda^*\varepsilon_G.
 \label{eq:localregret}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $M_1,M_2$ bound the first and second derivatives in Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth}. Put $f_\lambda(Q)=q_\alpha(H_\lambda QH_\lambda^\top)$.
For any symmetric perturbation $E$,
\[
 Df_\lambda(Q)[E]=Dq_\alpha(\Sigma_\lambda)[H_\lambda EH_\lambda^\top].
\]
Since $\|H_\lambda\|_\op^2\leq2$,
\begin{align*}
 \|H_\lambda EH_\lambda^\top-H_0EH_0^\top\|_\op&\leq3\lambda\|E\|_\op,\\
 \|\Sigma_\lambda-\Sigma_0\|_\op&\leq3B\lambda.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\[
 |D\{f_0-f_\lambda\}(Q)[E]|
 \leq(3M_1+6BM_2)\lambda\|E\|_\op.
\]
Integrate this derivative along the line segment from $Q$ to $Q'$, which remains in $\mathcal K_{\kappa,B}$. This proves \eqref{eq:paired} with $C=3M_1+6BM_2$. On the confidence event the set diameter is at most $2\varepsilon_G$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate} yields \eqref{eq:localregret}.
\end{proof}

If an analytic bound uses $\widehat g(\lambda)-C\lambda\varepsilon_G$ and its maximum is attained to tolerance $\tau$, choosing the anchor whenever the attained lower bound is negative preserves safety; the corresponding regret is at most $2C\lambda^*\varepsilon_G+\tau$.
Separate absolute critical-value intervals generally lose the factor $\lambda$. That loss matters when the beneficial weight approaches zero.

\section{A fully observable covariance confidence ball}
Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate} does not rely on a confidence set supplied by an oracle. Here is one explicit construction, implemented in \texttt{verify\_core.py}. It uses deliberately conservative constants; efficient multiplier calibration remains a separate numerical development.

Let the selection sample have size $n=n_G\geq2$. Assume $0\leq K_h\leq K_{\max}$ and $\pi\geq pc_e$. All statements are conditional on the independent training sample. Set $L=2J+1$ and define
\[
 X_i=\sqrt{p/v_h}\,(K_i,\,K_iU_{0,i}^\top,\,K_iB_i^\top)^\top,\qquad
 Q=\E X_iX_i^\top,\quad \widehat Q=n^{-1}\sum_iX_iX_i^\top.
\]
For $f\in[0,1]^J$, let
\[
 A_f=\begin{pmatrix}-f&I_J&0\\0&0&I_J\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $\Omega=A_{F_h}QA_{F_h}^\top$. Use
\[
 \widehat\mu=\mathbb P_GK_h,\qquad
 \widehat f_j=\left[\frac{\mathbb P_GK_hU_{0,y_j}}{\widehat\mu}\right]_{[0,1]},\qquad
 \widehat\Omega=A_{\widehat f}\widehat Q A_{\widehat f}^\top.
\]
The last matrix is automatically positive semidefinite.

Split $\delta$ into four positive parts $\delta_K,\delta_Q,\delta_N,\delta_\mu$.
Let $s_+$ be an exact one-sided Clopper--Pearson upper confidence bound, at error $\delta_K$, on $s=\Pp(K_h>0)$, computed from the number of nonzero kernels. Let
\[
 t_Q=\log(2L^2/\delta_Q),\quad t_N=\log(2J/\delta_N),\quad t_\mu=\log(2/\delta_\mu).
\]
Define
\begin{align*}
 M_X&=\frac{K_{\max}}{c_e\sqrt{pv_h}},&
 V_Q^+&=\frac{2K_{\max}^4s_+}{p c_e^3v_h^2},\\
 V_N^+&=\frac{2K_{\max}^2s_+}{pc_e},&
 V_\mu^+&=K_{\max}^2s_+,\\
 r_Q&=\sqrt{2V_Q^+t_Q/n}+\frac{4M_X^2t_Q}{3n},\\
 r_N&=\sqrt{2V_N^+t_N/n}+\frac{4K_{\max}t_N}{3npc_e},\\
 r_\mu&=\sqrt{2V_\mu^+t_\mu/n}+\frac{2K_{\max}t_\mu}{3n}.
\end{align*}
For $\widehat\mu>0$, put $\zeta=\min\{1,(r_N+r_\mu)/\widehat\mu\}$, and otherwise set $\widehat f_j=1/2$, $\zeta=1$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_G=(J+1)Lr_Q+
 2\sqrt{J(J+1)}\,\zeta\{\|\widehat Q\|_\op+Lr_Q\}.
 \label{eq:radius}
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Observable confidence ball]\label{prop:covball}
Under the stated boundedness, known-propensity, and sample-separation conditions,
\[
 \Pp\{\|\widehat\Omega-\Omega\|_\op\leq\varepsilon_G\mid\text{training}\}\geq1-\delta.
\]
For fixed $J$, bounded localization density, $\mu_h\asymp v_h$, and $n_Gpv_h\to\infty$, the radius has order
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_G=O_\Pp\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\log(J/\delta)}{N_G}}+
 \frac{\log(J/\delta)}{N_G}\right\},\qquad N_G=n_Gpv_h,
 \label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
with dimension-dependent constants. The candidates need not converge to the true regressions for this fitted-path confidence statement.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
When $R=0$, $|U_0|,|B|\leq1$; when $R=1$, both are at most $1/\pi$. Thus every raw scaled coordinate is bounded by $M_X$, and
\[
 \E\|X_i\|_\infty^4\leq\frac{2K_{\max}^4s}{p c_e^3v_h^2}.
\]
For an entry of $X_iX_i^\top$, the centered absolute bound is $2M_X^2$. Bernstein's inequality and a union bound over $L^2$ entries yield
$\|\widehat Q-Q\|_{\max}\leq r_Q$ on an event of error at most $\delta_Q+\delta_K$, using the event $s\leq s_+$.
The same reasoning gives simultaneous bounds $r_N$ on the numerator means and $r_\mu$ on the kernel mean, with the second-moment bounds displayed above. These statements can be intersected even though the upper support bound and moment estimates use the same sample: the inequalities with the true support probability are intersected with $s\leq s_+$ before substitution.

Writing $\E(K_hU_{0,y_j})=\mu_hF_h(y_j)$ gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\mathbb P_GK_hU_{0,y_j}}{\widehat\mu}-F_h(y_j)\right|
 \leq\frac{r_N+r_\mu}{\widehat\mu}.
\]
Clipping to $[0,1]$ cannot increase this error. Hence $\|\widehat f-F_h\|_\infty\leq\zeta$.
Now $\|A_f\|_\op\leq\sqrt{J+1}$,
$\|A_{\widehat f}-A_{F_h}\|_\op\leq\sqrt J\zeta$, and
$\|\widehat Q-Q\|_\op\leq Lr_Q$. Expanding
\[
 A_{\widehat f}\widehat Q A_{\widehat f}^\top-A_{F_h}QA_{F_h}^\top
\]
into a $\widehat Q-Q$ term and two $A_{\widehat f}-A_{F_h}$ terms gives \eqref{eq:radius}, using $\|Q\|_\op\leq\|\widehat Q\|_\op+Lr_Q$.
The union error is at most the four allocated errors. Under localization, $s_+=O_\Pp(v_h)$, $\widehat\mu\asymp_\Pp v_h$, and $\|\widehat Q\|_\op=O_\Pp(1)$ for fixed $J$, which gives \eqref{eq:rate}.
\end{proof}

The positive semidefinite operator-norm ball supplied by Proposition~\ref{prop:covball} is sufficient for Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate}. Intersecting it with $\mathcal K_{\kappa,B}$ invokes additional, explicitly stated covariance conditions for Theorem~\ref{thm:paired}; those conditions must not be silently imposed to exclude inconvenient data.

\section{Weak borrowing: an information bound and a matching certificate}
Consider a binary submodel with $S\in\{-1,1\}$ equally likely and
\[
 Z=\ind\{Y=0\},\qquad \Pp_\theta(Z=1\mid S)=1/2+\theta S,
 \quad |\theta|<1/4.
\]
The marginal target is $1/2$. Let $q=1/2$, $D=dS$ with fixed $0<d<1/4$, and $R\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$ independently. This is a valid binary-CDF augmentation family. Direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_\theta^2(\lambda)
 =p\Var_\theta(U_\lambda)
 =\frac14+(1-p)(d^2\lambda^2-2d\theta\lambda).
 \label{eq:binaryvar}
\end{equation}
For $0<\theta<d$, the oracle weight is $\lambda^*=\theta/d$, and the Gaussian critical-value improvement is
\begin{equation}
 g_\theta^*=z_{1-\alpha/2}\left\{\frac12-\sqrt{\frac14-(1-p)\theta^2}\right\}
 =z_{1-\alpha/2}(1-p)\theta^2+O(\theta^4).
 \label{eq:binarygain}
\end{equation}
For $\theta\leq0$, no positive weight improves the anchor.

\begin{theorem}[A local information obstruction]\label{thm:lower}
Suppose a rule issues a positive-gain certificate on an event $\mathcal A$, with $\Pp_0(\mathcal A)\leq\delta$.
From $n_G$ observations in the binary model, let $N_G=n_Gp$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \Pp_\theta(\mathcal A)\leq\delta+\sqrt{2N_G}\,|\theta|.
 \label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\theta=o(N_G^{-1/2})$, its probability of certifying the positive improvement is at most $\delta+o(1)$, while the optimal critical-value gain is $o(N_G^{-1})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The laws of $(S,R)$ are identical under $\theta$ and zero. Conditional on verification, the Bernoulli divergence to probability $1/2$ is at most its chi-squared divergence, $4\theta^2$. Thus the observed-data divergence satisfies
\[
 \KL(P_\theta^{n_G},P_0^{n_G})\leq4n_Gp\theta^2.
\]
Pinsker's inequality gives total variation at most $\sqrt{2N_G}|\theta|$. Apply the event inequality and then \eqref{eq:binarygain}.
To embed the construction locally, take $X$ independent of $S$, use a box kernel with support probability $r_h\asymp h^d$, and introduce the association $\theta$ only inside that support. All broadly observed variables have the same law under the two alternatives. The divergence bound replaces $N_G$ by $n_Gpr_h$, the expected local verified count.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Matching submodel certificate]\label{prop:matching}
Conditional on $k\geq1$ verified observations in the target neighborhood, let
\[
 \widehat\theta=\frac1{2k}\sum_{i=1}^k S_i(2Z_i-1),\qquad
 \theta_L=\widehat\theta-\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2k}},\qquad
 \widehat\lambda=\min\{1,(\theta_L)_+/d\}.
\]
The probability of selecting a strictly harmful positive weight is at most $\delta$.
For $\theta>0$, the adoption probability is at least $1-\beta$ whenever
\begin{equation}
 \theta\geq\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2k}}+
               \sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\beta)}{2k}}.
 \label{eq:matching}
\end{equation}
For $k=0$, use $\widehat\lambda=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each summand divided by two lies in $[-1/2,1/2]$ and has mean $\theta$.
One-sided Hoeffding bounds give $\Pp(\theta_L>\theta)\leq\delta$ and the adoption bound in \eqref{eq:matching}.
On the confidence event, if $\widehat\lambda>0$ then
$0<d\widehat\lambda\leq\theta_L\leq\theta$. Therefore
\[
 \sigma_\theta^2(\widehat\lambda)-\sigma_\theta^2(0)
 =(1-p)d\widehat\lambda(d\widehat\lambda-2\theta)
 \leq-(1-p)(d\widehat\lambda)^2<0.
\]
The Gaussian critical value is strictly increasing in the scalar variance, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

These results identify a fixed-submodel signal scale and its squared gain scale. They do not assert that every possible multi-threshold covariance geometry has the same detection boundary. They also concern certification of a specified estimator family, not a lower bound for estimating an otherwise known marginal target over this submodel. With $\varepsilon_G\asymp N_G^{-1/2}$ and $\lambda^*\asymp\theta$, Theorem~\ref{thm:paired} gives a regret scale $\theta N_G^{-1/2}$, consistent with the submodel boundary. Using a constant-in-$\lambda$ error bound would instead demand a gain of order $N_G^{-1/2}$ near the anchor and discard this adaptation.

\section{Selection and final simultaneous coverage}
A selection certificate and a coverage statement control different events. Keep training, selection, and final evaluation samples disjoint. No convergence of $\widehat\lambda$ to a unique deterministic minimizer is needed for the following transfer lemma.

\begin{theorem}[Protected-band construction]\label{thm:coverage}
Suppose \eqref{eq:confidence} holds and, conditional on training, the evaluation sample satisfies
\[
 \sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\sup_{t\in\R}
 \left|\Pp\left(\left\|n_E^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in E}H_\lambda\Psi(O_i)\right\|_\infty\leq t
 \middle|\text{training}\right)-\Pp(\|G_\lambda\|_\infty\leq t)\right|\leq\eta_E.
\]
Let $U_G(\lambda)=\sup_{Q\in\calC_G}c_Q(\lambda)$, and use \eqref{eq:rule}. Then the grid band
\[
 \widehat F_{E,\widehat\lambda}(y_j)\ \pm\
 \frac{U_G(\widehat\lambda)}{\widehat f_{h,E}\sqrt{n_Epv_h}}
\]
has simultaneous coverage at least $1-\alpha-\delta-\eta_E$. If the kernel denominator is zero, report the trivial $[0,1]$ band instead.
Moreover, its untruncated common half-width is no greater than that of the correspondingly protected anchor band using $U_G(0)$ and the same sample.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the confidence event, $U_G(\widehat\lambda)\geq c_\Omega(\widehat\lambda)$.
Given training and selection, the selected weight is fixed and the evaluation sample is independent. The approximation hypothesis gives conditional grid coverage at least $1-\alpha-\eta_E$, using the exact identity \eqref{eq:ratio}. Integration and the confidence-set error give the asserted bound.
For every $Q\in\calC_G$, optimality in \eqref{eq:rule} implies
$g_Q(\widehat\lambda)\geq\underline g_G(\widehat\lambda)\geq0$.
Hence $c_Q(\widehat\lambda)\leq c_Q(0)$ for every such $Q$, and taking suprema yields $U_G(\widehat\lambda)\leq U_G(0)$.
\end{proof}

The width comparison is with an anchor given the \emph{same} covariance protection, not with an uninflated conventional anchor. A desired total noncoverage probability must allocate the covariance-certificate and Gaussian-approximation errors as well as $\alpha$.
Alternatively, a uniformly valid independently calibrated evaluation-fold multiplier band gives marginal coverage $1-\alpha+o(1)$ irrespective of whether the selection certificate succeeds; its realized width ordering is then an empirical question. The joint event of coverage and true Gaussian gain has probability at least $1-\alpha-\delta-o(1)$.
Gaussian comparison, anti-concentration, and high-dimensional rectangle approximation are appropriate tools for controlling the remaining approximation terms \citep{cckanti,cckclt}.

The original rotating three-role estimator is not automatically covered by the above conditional argument: conditioning on all rotating selections can reveal information about several evaluation blocks. A replacement result must explicitly control this dependence or introduce an outer independent selection stage.

\subsection{From a threshold grid to an entire CDF}
Suppose simultaneous lower and upper limits $l_j,u_j$ cover $F_h(y_j)$. Then
\[
 l(y)=\max\{0,\max_{y_j\leq y}l_j\},\qquad
 u(y)=\min\{1,\min_{y_j\geq y}u_j\}
\]
cover $F_h(y)$ for every real $y$, with the natural empty-set conventions. The proof is monotonicity: each left grid value is a lower bound for the CDF to its right, and each right grid value is an upper bound to its left. Intersecting limits with $[0,1]$ preserves coverage. An inconsistent interval family off the coverage event can be replaced by $[0,1]$.
This extension provides whole-CDF coverage without a smooth density; it does not identify finite-grid width optimality with continuum-supremum optimality. A sharp growing-grid process result must account for interpolation error and deterioration of the fixed-grid perturbation constants.

\]

 \Psi^{\Delta}_{0,t}

and its correction block is the same $\sqrt{p/v_h}K_h B_t$ as before. Thus the entire paired-certification construction applies to the actual scientific difference, retaining all sensor--reference covariances.
